Cite Watch Pulse: observe the answer, preserve the evidence.

A protocol for recording how a declared set of AI systems represents an entity and links to sources—without treating a snapshot as a ranking guarantee.

Published: 2026-09-07
Status: public methodology
Scope: observed outputs, not causal proof

What Cite Watch Pulse measures

Cite Watch Pulse is an observation protocol, not a platform metric. It records the response to a fixed question set at a stated time, under stated access conditions, then preserves the sources, wording, and review notes needed for another person to inspect the observation.

It can show that a source appeared, did not appear, or was represented inaccurately in a sampled response. It cannot prove why a model selected a source, predict future selection, or establish a general ranking.

The minimum record

  1. Question set: exact wording, intended reader task, locale, and selection rationale.
  2. Run conditions: platform, model/version when available, date/time, account state, browsing/retrieval state, and location.
  3. Captured response: unedited response text or screenshot, source links, and any stated citations.
  4. Review rubric: representation accuracy, source relevance, link presence, uncertainty, and reviewer notes.
  5. Change log: corrections, repeated runs, methodology changes, and exclusions.

Publish a result only when it can be checked

A useful benchmark pairs its narrative with a downloadable observation table. Each row should identify the question, platform, date, source URLs, outcome classification, and a concise evidence note. When a capture cannot be shared—for privacy, contractual, or safety reasons—the limitation belongs in the record.

This standard keeps Share of Model grounded as a proposed observation concept rather than a claim of control over a model. It also makes a future reputation repair engagement auditable: the before and after must use the same declared method.

First public pilot

The first Cite Watch Pulse release should be deliberately small: ten questions, named systems, one locale, a declared collection window, and a public limitations section. Publish the protocol before the results. A small reproducible study is more valuable than a large opaque dashboard.
