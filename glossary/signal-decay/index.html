Signal Decay (SD)
The garden doesn't stay planted.

You plant a garden. Beautiful. Three months later you stop watering. The garden doesn't die overnight. It looks fine for weeks. Then one morning you walk out and everything is brown. Signal Decay is that brown morning. It was happening the whole time. You just didn't check.

Signal Decay
SD  /ˈsɪɡ.nəl dɪˈkeɪ/  ·  noun
The gradual erosion of an entity's citation frequency in AI-generated responses when fresh, machine-readable signals stop being published. Signal Decay is the inverse of signal freshness. it describes the rate at which an entity's visibility in AI outputs declines when the maintenance cost of that visibility is not paid.
Distinguished from content decay (SEO), which describes rankings dropping as competitors publish better content. Signal Decay describes citation frequency dropping as AI models update their understanding of a category with fresher signals. The process operates on different timelines and through different mechanisms than search ranking decay.
The Mechanism

Two clocks, two decay rates

AI systems cite entities through two mechanisms: training data and retrieval-augmented generation. Each decays on a different timeline.

01
Training Data Decay
Models like ChatGPT and Claude cite entities from their training data. snapshots of the web at a point in time. When a brand stops publishing, its training data representation freezes. The next model update includes fresher competitor content. The brand's relative citation frequency drops because the denominator grew while the numerator stayed flat. This decay is slow: model update cycles range from 3–12 months.
02
Retrieval Decay
Systems like Perplexity and ChatGPT with web access cite entities from live retrieval. they fetch fresh pages when answering. When a brand stops publishing, retrieval systems find fewer fresh pages associated with the entity. The brand's Share of Model drops within weeks, not months. This decay is fast and visible: a brand that goes quiet for 60 days will see measurable citation decline in retrieval-augmented systems.

The two clocks run simultaneously. A brand can hold training-data citations for months while losing retrieval citations in weeks. The combined effect is a decay curve that starts slow, accelerates, and then plateaus at a lower equilibrium. a new, lower Share of Model baseline.

The Curve

Signal Decay follows a predictable pattern

The decay curve has three phases. Understanding them determines when to intervene.

Phase 1: Latency
Duration: 0–30 days after signal stop
Retrieval systems still cite cached pages
Training data unaffected (model unchanged)
SoM appears stable. this is deceptive
→
Phase 2: Decline
Duration: 30–90 days
Retrieval systems find competitor content fresher
Citation frequency drops measurably
This is where most brands notice. too late
Phase 3: Equilibrium
After 90+ days without fresh signals, the entity settles at a new, lower Share of Model. The AI systems have recalibrated. Competitor signals now dominate the query set. Recovering lost SoM from this point costs 3–5x more effort than maintaining it would have. This is the compounding cost of stopping.

The latency phase is the cruelest. Everything looks fine. The numbers haven't moved. The brand celebrates. The decay is already running. It just hasn't reached the surface yet.

Related Concept

Signal Decay vs. Concept Drift. same instinct, different system

Machine learning has a well-documented phenomenon called concept drift. model accuracy degrades over time because the real-world distribution the model was trained on changes. The model's internal representation becomes stale.

Signal Decay is the mirror image. Instead of the model's representation going stale, the entity's external signals go stale. The model is fine. The entity stopped feeding it fresh evidence. The result is the same: the system produces worse answers about the entity.

The practical difference: concept drift is the model's problem to solve (retraining). Signal Decay is the entity's problem to solve (freshness). The ARM Framework's Mandate pillar exists specifically to address this. treating signal freshness as ongoing infrastructure, not a campaign.

One is the model's problem. The other is yours. The optimization target is different. The discipline required is the same: pay attention or pay the price.

Prevention

Five habits that prevent Signal Decay

Signal Decay is preventable. The cost of prevention is always lower than the cost of recovery. These five habits, drawn from the ARM Framework, maintain signal freshness.

01
Publish on a cadence
A consistent publishing rhythm. even one substantial piece per month. signals to retrieval systems that the entity is active. Volume matters less than regularity. A brand that publishes quarterly for three years has stronger signal freshness than one that publishes weekly for two months and stops.
02
Update existing content
Refreshing dateModified tags, updating statistics, and adding new sections to existing pages signals ongoing authority. AI systems weight recency. a page last updated six months ago signals less authority than one updated last week, even if the content is comparable.
03
Expand the citation network
New third-party mentions from authoritative sources keep the entity's citation graph growing. The Princeton GEO research showed that structured citation can boost visibility by up to 40%. Stale citation networks signal declining relevance.
04
Measure Share of Model monthly
Monthly SoM measurement catches decay in Phase 1. before the decline becomes visible to users. A 5% month-over-month drop is actionable. A 20% drop requires a recovery campaign.
05
Keep schema current
Update JSON-LD schema when the entity evolves. new products, new roles, new relationships. Stale schema that describes an entity as it was two years ago tells AI models the entity has not kept pace with its category.

Prevention is cheaper than recovery. Always. Every quarter. Without exception. The brands that understand this compound. The ones that don't pay 5x to get back what they lost.

FAQ

Common questions about Signal Decay

What is Signal Decay in GEO?
Signal Decay is the gradual erosion of an entity's citation frequency in AI-generated responses when fresh, machine-readable signals stop being published. When a brand stops publishing new content, updating schema, and building citation networks, AI models gradually displace it with fresher competitor signals.
How fast does Signal Decay happen?
Signal Decay operates on two timelines. Retrieval-augmented systems (Perplexity, ChatGPT with web access) can show decay within weeks of signal stagnation. Training-based knowledge decays more slowly, on the scale of model update cycles. months to years. A brand that stops all GEO activity will see citation frequency decline measurably within one quarter.
Is Signal Decay the same as concept drift?
Signal Decay shares DNA with concept drift. the machine learning phenomenon where model accuracy degrades as real-world distributions change. The difference: concept drift describes a model's internal accuracy degrading. Signal Decay describes an entity's external visibility degrading. The mechanisms are parallel, but the optimization target is different.
How do you prevent Signal Decay?
Prevent Signal Decay by maintaining signal freshness: publish consistently, update existing content with current data, expand your citation network with new third-party references, and keep schema markup current. The ARM Framework treats signal freshness as a compounding advantage, not a one-time task. Monthly Share of Model measurement catches decay early.
What is the relationship between Signal Decay and Share of Model?
Share of Model measures citation frequency at a point in time. Signal Decay describes the rate at which that frequency declines. Together they form a trend line: a brand gaining 2% SoM per month is compounding against decay. A brand that peaked at 40% SoM and is declining is experiencing Signal Decay.
