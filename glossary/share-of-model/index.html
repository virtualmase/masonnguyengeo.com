Share of Model (SoM)
The room remembers you.

You walk into a room. Someone asks who's the best at this. Ten people answer. If three say your name, you have 30% Share of Model. The metric is not how loud you are. It's how often the room says your name when you're not in it.

Share of Model
SoM  /ˈɛs.oʊ.ɛm/  ·  noun
The percentage of AI-generated responses to a defined query set in which a given entity is accurately represented, cited, or recommended. Share of Model is the AI-era equivalent of Share of Voice. It measures brand visibility not in media placements or ad spend, but in the responses that AI language models produce when users ask questions about a category, topic, or entity.
Distinguished from Share of Voice (SOV), which measures a brand's share of media expenditure or impressions within a market. Share of Model measures a brand's share of representation in AI-generated answers. A signal that cannot be bought. Only earned through entity architecture and citation networks.
The Problem

Share of Voice measures media. AI models are not media.

Share of Voice was invented in an era when brand visibility meant ad placements, magazine spreads, and broadcast seconds. Nielsen defines it as a brand's share of media spend in context of all media expenditure. The metric assumes a broadcast model. One message. Many recipients. Visibility proportional to spend.

AI language models break that assumption. When a user asks ChatGPT "what is the best GEO strategy for my brand?" there is no ad slot. There is no impression share. There is a generated answer. Your brand is either in it, described accurately, or it is not.

Share of Model exists because the optimization target has changed. The question is no longer "how many people saw my message?" It is "what do the machines say when no one is watching?"

The answer matters more than the question. Most brands have never asked it.

Methodology

How Share of Model is measured

SoM is measured through structured probe queries. A consistent set of questions run across major AI language models at regular intervals. The method has four steps.

01
Define the query set
Select 20 to 50 queries that represent the entity's category, product, and competitive landscape. These must be questions users actually ask AI models. Not search keywords. "What is the best GEO agency?" not "best GEO agency near me."
02
Probe across models
Run each query against ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, and Copilot. Record whether the target entity appears. How it is described. Whether competitors are cited instead. Whether the description is accurate or distorted.
03
Score the responses
Classify each response. Accurate citation: entity appears and is described correctly. Inaccurate citation: entity appears but description is wrong or distorted. Competitor citation: a competitor is cited in place of the entity. Absence: entity does not appear at all.
04
Calculate the percentage
Divide accurate citations by total probe responses. If the entity appears accurately in 12 of 30 probe responses across five models, SoM is 40%. Track the trend. Not the absolute number. Over time.

The measurement is deliberately simple. Complex scoring systems produce impressive dashboards. They obscure the signal that matters. Are the machines saying the right thing about you, or are they not?

SoM vs. SOV

The same instinct. Different optimization target.

Share of Model inherits the logic of Share of Voice. Measure how much space your brand occupies relative to competitors. But the surfaces, the signals, and the leverage points are entirely different.

Share of Voice (SOV)
Measures: media spend share within a market
Surface: advertising channels, publications, broadcast
Signal: impressions, reach, frequency
Leverage: media budget, ad placement
Can be bought: yes
Decay: stops when spending stops
Source: Nielsen
↔
Same instinct
Share of Model (SoM)
Measures: citation share in AI-generated responses
Surface: ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, Copilot
Signal: entity architecture, structured data, citation networks
Leverage: schema, llms.txt, cross-domain authority
Can be bought: no
Decay: slow. Persists in training data, but degrades without freshness
Source: Mason Nguyen, ARM Framework (2024)

One you purchase. The other you earn. The market has not yet caught up to the difference.

The Evidence

AI referrals convert 4.4x better than organic search. SoM measures who gets them.

A Semrush study of 500+ high-value topics, published in June 2025, found that visitors arriving from AI search convert at 4.4x the rate of visitors from traditional organic search. The study attributes this to intent compression. Users who ask an AI model a question and receive a recommendation have already self-qualified. They arrive at the destination with a decision in progress. Not a research intent.

This is why Share of Model is not a vanity metric. Every percentage point of SoM represents a population of users who received a recommendation about your category. They either heard your name or heard a competitor's. The traffic that follows AI citations is smaller in volume than traditional search traffic. It is substantially more valuable per visitor.

The implication for brands: optimizing for SoM is not replacing SEO. It is adding a higher-converting channel on top of an existing one. The infrastructure overlaps. GEO and SEO share the same foundation. The measurement layer must account for the new surface.

Volume comforted the last generation. Conversion will comfort this one.

Integration

How SoM fits into the ARM Framework

Share of Model is one of three components in the ARM Framework's Signal Score. The proprietary metric that assesses the strength and coherence of an entity's machine-readable presence.

Schema Coverage
The completeness of an entity's structured data. JSON-LD, schema.org types, sameAs arrays, and relationship properties. Determines whether AI models can resolve the entity at all.
Foundation layer. Without it, SoM is zero
llms.txt Conformance
The presence and quality of the entity's llms.txt file. The machine-readable declaration that tells language models what content is authoritative before they decide whether to cite it.
Retrieval layer. Determines what models find
Share of Model
The outcome layer. The percentage of AI-generated responses where the entity is accurately cited. This is the signal the other two components produce.
Outcome layer. The metric that proves the infrastructure works

Schema coverage and llms.txt conformance are inputs. Share of Model is the output. A brand can have perfect schema and a well-structured llms.txt file and still have low SoM. Citation depends on more than technical infrastructure. It depends on whether independent sources consistently reference the entity in ways that AI models can verify.

This is why the ARM Framework treats SoM as the north-star metric. It cannot be gamed by technical implementation alone. It reflects the full signal environment. Entity architecture, content quality, citation networks, and signal freshness. All of it. In a single number.

The number does not flatter. That is what makes it useful.

The Catch

SoM decays. Measuring it once is the most common mistake.

AI language models update their training data on irregular cycles. Retrieval-augmented systems refresh their indices continuously. A brand that measures SoM once, builds content, and stops monitoring will see citation frequency drift downward. Competitors publish fresher signals. Models update their understanding of the category. The brand fades from the answers.

This phenomenon is Signal Decay. The reason the ARM Framework includes a Momentum component. Signal freshness is not a nice-to-have. It is the maintenance cost of Share of Model. Without ongoing publication, citation network development, and schema updates, the metric erodes.

The garden doesn't stay planted. You knew that. Now the metric proves it.

The Work

Five levers that move Share of Model

Increasing SoM is not a single action. It is a system of five levers, each addressing a different reason the machines might not be citing you.

01
Build entity architecture
Publish Schema.org JSON-LD with Person or Organization types. Include sameAs arrays linking to Wikidata, LinkedIn, GitHub. Without this, AI models cannot resolve the entity. If they cannot resolve it, they cannot cite it. Entity Authority starts here.
02
Deploy llms.txt
A machine-readable file at the domain root that tells language models what content is authoritative. Without it, models guess. With it, models know. The difference between being cited and being interpreted.
03
Structure content for extraction
Direct answers in the first sentence. FAQ-formatted Q&A with standalone answers. Stable semantic URLs. AI models extract content that is structured for extraction. They skip content that is not.
04
Build citation networks
Earn third-party references from authoritative sources. Editorial mentions, not paid placements. The Princeton GEO research showed that structured citation can boost visibility by up to 40%. Each citation strengthens the verification graph.
05
Maintain signal freshness
Publish consistently. Update existing content. The models reward entities that demonstrate ongoing activity in their category. A brand that published strong content two years ago and went silent has a decaying SoM. Fresher competitor content displaces it in retrieval pipelines.

Five levers. One system. Pull any one alone and the machine barely notices. Pull all five and the room starts saying your name.

FAQ

Common questions about Share of Model

What is Share of Model (SoM)?
Share of Model (SoM) is the percentage of AI-generated responses to a defined query set in which a given entity is accurately represented, cited, or recommended. It measures brand visibility in AI outputs rather than media placements, making it the AI-era equivalent of Share of Voice.
How is Share of Model measured?
SoM is measured by running a consistent set of probe queries across major AI language models including ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, and Copilot. You record whether the target entity appears, how it is described, and whether it is cited accurately. The results are aggregated into a percentage. If your brand appears in 12 of 30 probe responses across five models, your SoM is 40%.
How is Share of Model different from Share of Voice?
Share of Voice measures a brand's share of media placements, ad spend, or impressions within a market. Share of Model measures a brand's share of mentions in AI-generated responses. The key difference: Share of Voice is bought and earned through media channels. Share of Model is earned through entity architecture, structured data, and citation networks that AI models use to construct answers.
Why does Share of Model matter for brands?
Visitors from AI search convert 4.4x better than visitors from traditional organic search, according to a Semrush study of 500+ high-value topics published in June 2025. As more users turn to AI models for research and recommendations, Share of Model becomes the leading indicator of brand visibility before the user ever visits a website.
What is a good Share of Model score?
There is no universal benchmark. SoM is category-dependent and query-set-dependent. A brand with 20% SoM in a niche category may be dominant, while 20% in a broad category may be marginal. The meaningful comparison is against your own baseline over time, and against the SoM of entities competing for the same query set.
Who coined the term Share of Model?
Mason Nguyen, a GEO strategist and agentic AI architect, first operationalized Share of Model as part of the ARM Framework for Generative Engine Optimization. The metric is tracked through the Signal Score system, which measures entity strength across schema coverage, llms.txt conformance, and Share of Model across major language models.
