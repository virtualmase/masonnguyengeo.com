The ARM Framework

Authority · Retrieval · Mandate — a working method for connecting public identity, usable evidence, and accountable decisions.

Form: working framework
Scope: public and AI-assisted systems
Method: inspectable evidence
Status: complete
Working boundary. ARM is Mason Nguyen’s own operating framework, not a standard, certification, or claim that any model or search system will retrieve, cite, or represent a source in a particular way.
01 / The problem

Make the path from claim to decision inspectable.

ARM stands for Authority, Retrieval, and Mandate. It is a working framework for building systems in which a claim can be traced to an identity, supported by evidence, made available through an understandable path, and bounded by a responsible decision owner.

Most public systems fail in the seams. An organization may publish useful material without making its authorship clear. A knowledge base may be accurate but difficult to retrieve or maintain. An agent may produce a plausible answer without a record of what it used, what it was allowed to decide, or when it should stop. ARM treats those as one connected design problem rather than separate content, technical SEO, and automation problems.

The framework is intentionally narrower than a universal theory of AI governance. Its purpose is to improve the legibility of a public or operational system: who is speaking, what supports the statement, how someone or something can find that support, and who retains responsibility for the next action.

02 / The framework

Three layers, one review loop.

The terms are sequential in use but iterative in practice. A weak mandate creates ambiguous evaluation. Weak authority makes evidence hard to interpret. Weak retrieval leaves strong evidence inaccessible at the moment it is needed.

A / Authority

What can this source responsibly say?

Authority is not a popularity score. It is a clear identity, an evidence base, and an honest account of scope. The practical question is whether a reader can distinguish the source, its expertise, its evidence, and its limitations.

R / Retrieval

Can the right evidence be found and checked?

Retrieval is the route between a question and a maintainable source. It includes information architecture, precise language, structured data where appropriate, durable URLs, citations, and current source records.

M / Mandate

Who may decide, act, or escalate?

Mandate names the job, constraints, decision owner, and escalation path. It prevents a system from treating an available answer as permission to take an unbounded action.

03 / Authority

Authority is a record of identity and evidence.

Within ARM, authority begins with specificity. The source should have a stable name, a clear subject, a coherent set of public references, and claims proportionate to the evidence available. It is not a shortcut for reputation, rankings, or a promise of citation.

For a web property, the practical work includes clear authorship, accurate organization and person information, sources that show where a claim came from, and content that distinguishes a definition, interpretation, experiment, or opinion. Google describes structured data as explicit clues about a page’s meaning and says the markup should describe visible content accurately; it also cautions against incomplete or misleading implementation.[3]

That makes structured data useful but bounded. It can clarify a page’s contents and relationships. It cannot by itself establish expertise, make a claim true, or determine how a particular model will represent a source.

04 / Retrieval

Retrieval is not visibility theater.

Retrieval means that evidence can be located, interpreted, and revisited. On the public web, that often starts with a useful page hierarchy, descriptive titles, plain-language definitions, references, consistent entity naming, and pages that make their own scope obvious. In a product or agent workflow, it also includes the source set, update process, permissions, and how a response can disclose its basis.

The RAG literature is a useful reminder that an external memory path is not equivalent to solved provenance. Lewis and colleagues describe retrieval-augmented generation as combining model parameters with a retrievable non-parametric memory, while noting that provenance and knowledge updating remain open problems.[4] ARM therefore asks teams to treat retrieval as an evidence path to inspect, not as a black-box promise of factuality.

The relevant question is not “Will a machine find us?” It is “If this claim is found, can a person identify the source, inspect the support, see the date and limits, and decide whether it fits the current task?”

05 / Mandate

Mandate turns available information into bounded action.

A mandate says what a system is trying to do, for whom, under which constraints, with which authority, and when it must return control to a person. This applies equally to a publishing workflow, a research assistant, a support agent, or a human team making a decision from an evidence base.

NIST’s AI Risk Management Framework treats governance as a cross-cutting activity and organizes risk work around governing, mapping, measuring, and managing. Its core stresses documented roles, context, review, and iterative risk management rather than a one-time checklist.[2] ARM does not duplicate that framework. It uses the same practical discipline at a smaller operational scale: every meaningful action needs a stated purpose, an evidence boundary, and a named owner for escalation.

Mandate test: If a system cannot state what it is allowed to do, what it is not allowed to do, and who owns the decision when confidence is insufficient, the mandate is not yet complete.
06 / Working sequence

Start with a decision, then build the record around it.

  1. Write the mandate. Name the decision, audience, expected output, constraints, risk tolerance, and human owner. Do this before choosing a model, a tool, or a publishing channel.
  2. Inventory the authority record. Identify the source entity, the claims it can support, the primary evidence, dependencies, conflicts, publication dates, and known gaps.
  3. Design the retrieval path. Make the authoritative material easy to locate and inspect. Use coherent terminology, stable URLs, linked references, version cues, and structured data that matches visible content.
  4. Test against the mandate. Review whether the retrieved material actually answers the designated question, whether the context is current, and whether the output remains within the allowed action boundary.
  5. Record the result and improve the next pass. Preserve the source set, decision, uncertainty, escalation, and feedback. The point is not to generate more outputs; it is to leave behind a more useful system state.

08 / A minimum viable record

What to document before calling a system accountable.

A team does not need a large program to start. It needs one consequential workflow and a record that can survive handoffs. For a public article, that may be author, claim, source, update date, and related definitions. For an agent workflow, it may include the prompt class, approved sources, tool permissions, output format, evaluator, and escalation condition. The Applied Systems Build Record shows how selected public artifacts can name their present phase, visible evidence, and operational boundary without turning an interface into a result claim.

  • A named entity and a concise statement of what the system or source is for.
  • Claims separated from evidence, opinion, and unanswered questions.
  • A maintained source set with dates, ownership, and known limitations.
  • A retrievable path that someone else can inspect without private context.
  • A mandate that names allowed actions, disallowed actions, and escalation conditions.
  • A review cadence and a place to record feedback, failures, and changes.

These are implementation prompts, not a compliance declaration. NIST describes its AI RMF as voluntary and intended to help organizations incorporate trustworthiness considerations into the design, development, use, and evaluation of AI systems.[1] The appropriate controls depend on the domain, harm profile, laws, data, and system capabilities involved.

09 / FAQ

Questions that keep the method honest.

Is ARM a claim about how all AI systems work?

No. Different search engines, models, retrieval systems, and workflows have different architectures and policies. ARM is a way to design and review a source system without assuming a particular platform outcome.

Does structured data make a source authoritative?

No. Accurate structured data can clarify a page’s meaning and support machine interpretation. It should reflect visible content and be validated, but it does not establish credibility or guarantee a rich result.[3]

What makes a mandate usable?

A usable mandate names the task, constraints, evidence boundary, decision owner, and escalation point. If those are missing, a confident-looking output can still be operationally ambiguous.
