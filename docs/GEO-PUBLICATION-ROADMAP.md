# GEO publication roadmap

## Editorial standard

Every indexable page must help a reader complete a real task and make its claims easy to verify. A page is not published merely to target a phrase or establish an association.

Each published research page needs:

- A plain-language answer in the opening section and one descriptive H1.
- A named author, publication date, and substantive revision date.
- Primary-source citations for factual or time-sensitive claims.
- One self-canonical URL, a crawlable internal-link path, and page-specific structured data that matches visible content.
- An explanatory visual with descriptive alt text when a visual materially improves comprehension.
- A related-reading path that takes readers to a definition, method, or evidence source.

## Publication sequence

1. Maintain the three pillar pages: What Is GEO, ARM Framework, and AI Visibility Strategy.
2. Publish the methodology for measuring Share of Model before publishing any benchmark results.
3. Publish a dated AI Visibility Benchmark with its prompt set, model/version, query date, sources, limitations, and raw observations.
4. Publish practical tools and checklists only when their instructions have been tested on a real implementation.
5. Turn original research into external references: a public GitHub repository, presentations, interviews, and editorial contributions that link back to the underlying method.

## Measurement

Review monthly:

- Search Console: index coverage, impressions, clicks, query/page pairs, and technical errors.
- Content: organic entrances, engagement, tool/template use, and assisted inquiry paths.
- Authority: relevant independent referring domains and citations to primary research pages.
- Answer engines: a fixed, versioned prompt set across supported models; record the exact response, source links, date, and model version.

Do not report citation or ranking outcomes as guarantees. Treat them as measured observations with documented methodology.
